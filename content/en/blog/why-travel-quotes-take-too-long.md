Ask any agent running a boutique travel agency what eats their week, and quotes usually come up first. Not because the work itself is hard, but because most of it gets redone from zero every single time.

## The real cost isn't the writing, it's the rebuilding

A typical quote pulls together a handful of services: a hotel, a guide, transport, maybe an activity or two. None of that is complicated on its own. The time goes into reassembling it: opening last month's file, renaming it, deleting the old client's details, checking the prices are still current, recalculating the margin by hand, and hoping nothing was missed.

Do that for every client, every season, every destination, and the hours add up fast, especially when a client asks for two or three variations before deciding.

## Margins get recalculated by hand, and that's where mistakes hide

Purchase price from the supplier, margin, sales price shown to the client: three numbers that have to stay consistent across every line of a quote. In a spreadsheet, that consistency depends entirely on formulas nobody re-checks under deadline pressure. A wrong cell reference, a margin applied to the wrong line, and the agency either underprices a trip or sends a client a price that doesn't match what was agreed on the phone.

## The document itself sends a signal

A quote is often the first real document a prospective client sees from an agency. A layout that looks different every time, or a PDF that was clearly built by hand, works against the professionalism the agency is trying to project, even when the trip being sold is excellent.

## What changes with a catalog behind the quote

The fix isn't writing faster. It's not rebuilding at all. When services, prices and suppliers live in one place and a quote is built by picking from that catalog rather than retyping it, three things happen at once:

- The margin is computed automatically from the purchase price, so it can't be forgotten or miscalculated under pressure.
- Every quote uses the same layout, so the document looks consistent whether it's the agency's first client or its five hundredth.
- A second or third variation for the same client takes minutes, because most of the quote is reused, not rewritten.

None of this removes the judgment an agent brings to a trip. It just gets the repetitive part out of the way, so that judgment goes into the parts of the job that actually need it.
